The result

First on both core Vaughan keywords

Rank is not one number, and it is not one map either. A profile can own the searches that name its own city and sit well down the list on terms that reach past it, so the honest read is keyword by keyword, inside the area the scan actually covered.

Below is the table of the LeadSnap scan results for Everfinish. The scan runs on a grid clipped to the Vaughan municipal boundary, so every figure on this page is a Vaughan figure. The challenge

Refinishing is won inside the map pack

Somebody decides to refinish the kitchen instead of replacing it. They search, look at the three businesses at the top of the map, and call one. Everything below that block gets a fraction of the attention. For a trade that sells a single high consideration job rather than repeat small ones, those three slots are most of the channel.

Cabinet refinishing also has a category problem. It sits next to general painting, kitchen renovation and cabinet supply in Google's eyes, and searchers use all of those words for the same job. A profile filed under the wrong primary category competes for the wrong searches. So does one that never says the work in the words customers type.

Then there is the geography. Vaughan covers a lot of ground and the population is spread across communities that behave like separate towns. Ranking near the shop proves very little. The only honest read is to scan the whole municipal area point by point. Then look at where the coverage thins out.

What we did

The levers we actually pulled

Google Business Profile first. Primary category, secondary categories, service list, service area, hours, description. All of it set the way Google reads it, not the way an owner would write it. The services are named as the jobs customers search for. Photos go up on a schedule. Posts go out on a schedule. The profile gets worked every week, not filled in once and left alone.

Service pages second. A page for every job the company actually sells, written so each one targets a distinct search rather than restating the page beside it. Near duplicate pages do not rank and do not convert, so the copy is built from the real scope of the work.

Area pages third. A page for each community inside the service area, researched from real local sources rather than spun from a template with the name swapped out. This is the part that moves coverage in the corners of a grid, and it is the part most often skipped.

Citations fourth. Name, address and phone matched across the directory set so nothing on the open web contradicts the profile. Inconsistent listings are quiet drag, and they are cheap to fix once the profile is settled.

Interlinking and indexing throughout. Schema on every page. A clean internal link silo from the home page down to each service and each area page. An XML sitemap. Index submission every time a page ships. A page Google has not indexed cannot rank, so this runs as part of the build rather than as a later cleanup.
